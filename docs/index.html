The Tim Ballard Defamation League

mimesis

Forensic comparison of videos, pictures and transcripts: what is the same, what was cut, and who posted it first. Every test is published before it runs, so nobody can move the goalposts afterwards.

What it was made for

Mimesis is Greek for imitation. The toolkit was started in June 2025 for one job: Tim Ballard tells the same story again and again, and we wanted to line the tellings up, word for word.

The first run compared four tellings of his border-wall story. A 2025 White House video that rolled out the same script, against three from 2019:

  • his Deseret News op-ed, Feb 4, 2019: “I’ve fought sex trafficking at the border. This is why we need a wall”
  • his Fox News op-ed: “I’ve fought sex trafficking as a DHS special agent. We need to build the wall for the children”
  • the 2019 White House meeting on human trafficking and the border, where he was one of the speakers (transcript, The American Presidency Project)

Whisper transcribes the video, the articles’ text is pulled from the page, and the versions are set side by side: what stays, what changes, what gets cut. The first script is still in the history: compare_sources.py, June 25, 2025.

How we work

Inputs are hashed and published with the code and the decision rule; the commit time is the timestamp. Then the published command runs, word for word, and the result goes in its own file. The code is in the mimesis repository.
